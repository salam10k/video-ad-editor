If you're googling **plumber for running toilet** at 11 p.m. instead of sleeping, welcome. Your toilet has been hissing longer than most celebrity marriages, and you're starting to take the sound personally. Good news: you're probably not calling anyone tonight.

A running toilet is almost always one of three small parts misbehaving. Let's find the culprit, fix it, and figure out when you actually need an [emergency plumber](/en/services/emergency-plumbing-riyadh/).

## Why your toilet keeps running: the 3 usual suspects

Inside the tank live the stars of this drama:

1. **The flapper:** the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. It hardens or warps with age, stops sealing, and water sneaks into the bowl. This is the culprit most of the time.
2. **The float:** set too high, the water reaches the overflow tube and drains forever.
3. **The fill valve:** when it fails, water keeps coming in even when the tank is full. That's the famous hiss.

> Quick rule: water refilling every few minutes = probably the flapper. Constant, non-stop flow = probably the float or fill valve.

## The food colouring test: find a silent leak in 15 minutes

Before you take anything apart, confirm the leak:

- Add 5 drops of food colouring to the **tank** (not the bowl, we believe in you).
- Don't flush for 15 minutes.
- Colour in the bowl means the flapper isn't sealing. Congratulations, you caught it.

The US EPA recommends the same test in its [Fix a Leak Week guide](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/fix-leak-week), because toilet leaks are some of the most ignored water wasters in any home.

## Toilet flapper replacement, step by step

You need: a new flapper (bring the old one to the store to match it), gloves, and 30 minutes.

1. Turn off the shut-off valve behind the toilet.
2. Flush to empty the tank.
3. Unhook the old flapper and the chain.
4. Fit the new one and clip the chain with a little slack (not tight, not dangling).
5. Turn the water back on and test.

Silence? Go back to bed. Still running? Keep reading.

## Toilet float adjustment when water hits the overflow tube

Check the water level. If it's above the overflow tube, lower the float: turn the adjustment screw or slide the clip down the rod. Aim for about 2–3 cm below the top of the tube.

If you like knowing how things work, here's how a [flush toilet works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flush_toilet).

## When to call a plumber for a running toilet

Call if:

- You replaced the flapper and adjusted the float and it still runs.
- There's water on the floor around the base (that's not an annoying noise anymore, that's a floor repair waiting to happen).
- The tank itself is cracked.
- You simply don't have the time or the mood, which is a perfectly respectable reason.
