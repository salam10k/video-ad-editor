**How much does consulting cost?** Ask a consultant and you'll hear the two most expensive words in business: "It depends." Which is true, but also deeply unhelpful when you're trying to put a number in a budget before Thursday.

So here are actual numbers: typical hourly rates, project prices and retainers, the five ways consultants charge, and the red flags that tell you you're about to overpay. If you'd rather skip ahead and talk to a human, here are our [consulting services in Riyadh](/en/services/consulting-riyadh/).

## How much does consulting cost? The short answer

For most small and mid-sized businesses, consulting lands in these ranges:

| How you pay | Typical range (USD) | In Saudi riyals (≈ ×3.75) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per hour | $100 – $400 | SAR 375 – 1,500 |
| Per project | $5,000 – $75,000 | SAR 19k – 280k |
| Monthly retainer | $3,000 – $25,000 | SAR 11k – 94k |

These are typical US market ranges published by advisory firms in 2026; on marketplaces like Clutch, the [average business consulting rate](https://clutch.co/consulting/pricing) is lower, around $25–$49 per hour, because it includes many smaller firms and lower-cost countries. Local markets vary a lot: in Saudi Arabia, single one-hour management consultations are often sold as a package, so always ask what the hour actually includes.

At the very top, the famous brands play a different sport. Public US government rate cards show a McKinsey senior partner billing about $1,194 per hour, as [this breakdown of McKinsey, BCG and Bain fees](https://slideworks.io/resources/management-consulting-fees-how-mc-kinsey-prices-projects) shows. You probably don't need that for your HR policy. (If you do, congratulations on the business.)

## The 5 consulting pricing models (and which one protects you)

Every consulting fee is one of these five, or a mix:

![Bar chart comparing consulting pricing models from hourly (least predictable) to fixed fee (most predictable)](/images/consulting-cost-models.svg)

1. **Hourly rate.** You pay for time. Simple, flexible, and the meter is always running. Fine for short questions; risky for open-ended projects unless there's a written ceiling.
2. **Daily or weekly rate.** Common when the consultant works with your team for long stretches. More predictable than hourly.
3. **Fixed fee.** One price for a defined result. This is what the big strategy firms prefer, and it's usually safest for you, because the total is capped.
4. **Monthly retainer.** A recurring fee for ongoing access: think "advisor on call". Great for continuous support, useless if you never call.
5. **Value or outcome-based.** A lower base fee plus a share of the measurable result (money saved, revenue gained). Aligns incentives, but only works when the result is easy to measure.

> Best tip in this article: when the scope is clear, ask for a **fixed fee with milestone payments**. When it isn't, buy a small paid discovery first, then get a fixed fee for the rest.

## Business consultant cost by type of consulting

Not all consulting is priced the same. Typical US ranges for small businesses:

- **Strategy consulting:** $200–$500 per hour, or roughly $10,000–$50,000 for a defined strategy project. You're paying for big decisions: markets, growth, pricing, expansion.
- **Financial / CFO consulting:** $150–$400 per hour, or $3,000–$15,000 a month for ongoing part-time CFO support. Often the best return for growing companies that outgrew their bookkeeper.
- **Marketing consulting:** $100–$300 per hour, or $2,000–$15,000 a month. Watch the line between *advice* and *execution*: running the ads is a different budget.
- **Operations consulting:** $150–$400 per hour, or $5,000–$25,000 for a process improvement project. The savings are usually measurable, which makes outcome-based pricing possible.
- **HR consulting:** priced by compliance risk and rollout size. A policy review is cheap; a company-wide restructuring is not.

Whatever the type, the same rule applies: the clearer your question, the smaller the bill.

## What's actually included in a consulting price?

Two quotes with the same number can buy very different things. Before comparing, check whether the price includes:

- **Discovery:** interviews, data review and site visits, or just one call?
- **A written deliverable:** a report or plan you keep, or only verbal advice?
- **Implementation support:** do they help you execute, or hand over slides and leave?
- **Follow-up:** a review meeting 30 or 60 days later to check the plan is working?
- **Expenses:** travel, tools and data subscriptions are often billed on top.

A cheaper quote that excludes the written plan and follow-up is not cheaper. It's just shorter.

## What affects consulting fees?

Same title, very different invoices. The price moves with:

- **Experience and specialty.** A tax or M&A specialist charges more than a generalist, because mistakes in their field cost more.
- **Scope and complexity.** Reviewing one process is not redesigning the whole company.
- **Urgency.** "We need it by Sunday" has a price, and it's not a discount.
- **Who actually does the work.** Big firms sell a partner in the pitch and analysts in the delivery. Ask who will be in the room.
- **Deliverables.** A one-hour call, a written report and hands-on implementation are three different products.
- **Location.** Rates in New York, London, Riyadh and online marketplaces differ sharply.

## Consultant hourly rate: is $100 an hour good for consulting?

It depends on what you're buying (sorry, we promised not to say that). Here's the honest version: **$100 an hour is the low end** for professional consulting. It can be perfectly fair for a generalist, a junior consultant or a small, well-defined task.

But a cheap hour can be the expensive option. A consultant at $100/hour who needs 40 hours costs $4,000. An expert at $300/hour who solves the same problem in 8 hours costs $2,400, and you got your answer a month earlier.

So don't compare hourly rates. Compare **total cost for the result you need**.

## Consulting retainer vs fixed fee consulting: which should you choose?

Use this quick test:

- **Clear deliverable** ("a written pricing strategy", "a new org chart") → fixed fee.
- **Ongoing need** ("someone to review decisions every month") → retainer, with response times and included hours in writing.
- **Unclear problem** ("sales are dropping and we don't know why") → small paid discovery first, then fixed fee.
- **Measurable upside** ("cut procurement costs") → base fee plus a performance bonus.

## Red flags that mean you're overpaying

The competitors' guides mostly explain how consultants *set* prices. Here's the part written for the person paying:

- **No written scope.** If deliverables aren't on paper, the bill has no ceiling.
- **Hourly billing with no cap** on a project that could run for months.
- **The senior expert disappears** after the contract is signed.
- **A long proposal about the firm, a short one about your problem.**
- **Pressure to sign this week** "before prices go up".
- **No way to measure success.** If neither side can say what "done" looks like, you'll keep paying until someone gets tired.

## How to get more value from every consulting hour

You can cut the bill without cutting the quality:

1. **Prepare before the first session.** Numbers, documents, the decision you need to make. Every hour spent explaining basics is an hour you pay for.
2. **Write the question in one sentence.** "Should we open a second branch in Jeddah this year?" is cheaper to answer than "help us grow".
3. **Start small.** A paid discovery or one session tells you whether the consultant is good before you sign the big contract.
4. **Agree on deliverables and dates in writing.**
5. **Ask for the plan to be usable without them.** A good consultant leaves you independent, not dependent.
6. **Negotiate the structure, not just the price.** Instead of asking for 20% off, ask for milestone payments, a fixed cap, or a smaller first phase. Consultants say yes to those far more often, and they protect you better than a discount.

## The bottom line

So, how much does consulting cost? Typically $100–$400 an hour, $5,000–$75,000 a project, or $3,000–$25,000 a month on retainer, with famous brands far above that. The number matters less than the structure: clear scope, a capped price, and a way to measure the result.
